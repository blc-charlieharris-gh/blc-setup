On SiteScore audits ([[project_sitescore_audits]]), Charlotte caught two related over-claims in the Stellar Insulation draft ([[project_sitescore_stellar]]):

1. **"ECO4 runs to 31 Dec 2026, so you have ~5 months left to sell"** is an assumption, not a fact. **A scheme's window being open is NOT the same as that installer still having supplier/obligation funding available.** Funding position is invisible from outside the business. The cost-of-gap maths was also built on that 5.5-month window, which made the whole £ figure rest on the assumption.
2. **Prescribing the service is also an assumption.** Seeing MCS solar certification and a boiler-only homepage, the draft implied "you should push solar". But they might want to *go into* solar, stay in ECO4, chase Warm Homes Plan, or something we haven't thought of. Charlotte: "we can't assume the service, maybe they want to go into solar."

**Why:** these audits go live to prospects and their credibility rests on the line between verified and inferred. Guessing the strategy also produces the wrong brief, and you end up building the wrong website twice. It's worse than useless as sales: it tells the owner we didn't listen.

**How to apply:**
- **State what you verified, ask the rest.** "What we do know is the site is out of date and cannot be attracting the homeowners you want" (Charlotte's framing) is the defensible claim. Which homeowner they want is *their* answer and becomes Stage 0 of the roadmap: a short conversation, explicitly "the one input the website can't give us".
- **Use the either-way construction** for any time-sensitive scheme claim: if the funding is live, the stale date is costing real jobs; if it isn't, the site is advertising what they can't supply, which is worse. Both roads → fix it. No assumption needed, and it's *more* persuasive than the confident version.
- **De-couple the cost box from any scheme assumption.** Jobs/month × margin × 12 works regardless of what they sell. Always label it a conservative illustration and ask them to sense-check the margin.
- Word-frequency and scroll-depth of the live page ("boiler" 68x vs "solar" 4x; services 92% down the page) are *facts* and make the point without prescribing anything: the site has picked a positioning by default, whether or not it's the one they want.
- Related bias, same family as [[feedback_sitescore_unbiased_scoring]]: don't let a tidy thesis ("they should sell solar!") drive the findings. Judge and report what's there.

Also from this round: **put the score in the hero, above the fold**, not only in the section below the exec summary.
