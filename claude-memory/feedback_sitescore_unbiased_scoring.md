On SiteScore audits ([[project_sitescore_audits]]), Charlotte caught that every audit rated the site's design "ok" (design was always the top row). That was a real bias, from three habits: the template thesis "looks good but converts poorly" needs design to score high; opening with praise softens the client before the price; and design is subjective so it defaulted generous.

**Why:** the audits go live to clients and their credibility depends on honest, independent scoring, not a house style that flatters design.

**How to apply:** score design, build/backend, and copy each on their own merits with NO prior weighting toward any outcome. No reflexive "design is the soft landing", and equally don't slash it, judge what's actually there. A strong brand can score well while the same site's execution (AI-composite imagery, flat baked-in graphics, poster-not-webpage layout) drags the design score down honestly. Drop "conversion-first weighting" language from the scorecard lead; say each area is judged independently.

Related: HQ Group audit rework, the real fix for a confusing multi-audience site is audience-specific subdomains (B2B group site vs B2C consumer site), NOT forcing renewables onto the group homepage, because a homeowner from an ad landing on a corporate org chart is an instant disconnect and every lead checks the site.
